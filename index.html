Illustrative evidence pattern. Use the physical chromatogram prepared at the booth as the actual evidence.

Experiment instructions

  1. Place a small ink spot near the pencil baseline on chromatography paper.
  2. Stand the paper in a shallow layer of water, keeping the ink above the waterline.
  3. Allow the solvent to travel up the paper.
  4. Compare the separated pigment bands with the reference strips.

What to observe

Different dyes travel different distances because they interact differently with the paper and solvent.

Look for the same number, colour, and relative position of pigment bands. Do not rely on colour alone.

SAFETY NOTICE: The Briggs–Rauscher reaction must be prepared and operated exclusively by the chemistry teacher. Visitors observe the demonstration and record the results. Do not mix, pour, or handle the reagents.

What to observe

  • Notice the sequence of clear or pale, amber, and blue-black.
  • Watch whether the colour changes happen repeatedly.
  • Count each complete return through the repeating colour pattern.

Chemistry file

The Briggs–Rauscher reaction is a chemical oscillating reaction. During the reaction, the concentrations of chemical species change over time, producing repeated visible colour changes.

Its key feature is repeated colour oscillation—not simply one sudden colour change like a conventional clock demonstration.

Record at least one complete colour cycle. Count only a full sequence of colour changes, not each individual colour as a separate cycle.
